## What it builds
The website a good company would ship: it says plainly what is sold, shows the real thing on every screen, and looks made for this client, not from a template and not like an art piece. figma.com and x.ai are the bar.

## House rules
These nine win over everything else in this skill.
1. **No caption or description under any image, render or visual.** Licence credits go in one footer line.
2. **Every visual renders, never an empty ground,** at 1440, 1024, 768 and 390.
3. **One section per screen:** the hero alone on the first screen, every section exactly one or two screens tall at 1440x900, no second section heading peeking in.
4. **Equal left and right content edges.**
5. **Hover micro-animations:** a linked card lifts 2px, a photo zooms 1.02-1.03 inside its frame, buttons step on hover and press on click, underlines draw, card loops cycle in 2.5-4 s, repeat and restart on hover. The motion layer does it; never strip or override it. No tilt, carousels or timed tabs.
6. **More and better real photographs where they fit;** nicer backgrounds; better heroes, layouts and fonts.
7. **Real full-colour icons** (a known product's real icon, never a drawn one); small diagram visuals in cards (a hub with branches; one input, a main processor, three parallel lanes; pulses running along the lines).
8. **The big award-site scenes and creative moments are part of the skill,** but shader effects only where the trade has a technical subject (tech, AI, developer tools, gaming, fintech, hardware), never on a local cafe, bakery, guesthouse or shop: those get photographs.
9. **Addresses:** street and district only (no street given: the district), no house number, postcode or coordinates; no register or VAT numbers; no phone in a real format; contacts on `.example`.
